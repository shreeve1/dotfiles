# Current Challenges

## First Management Role
Historically a technical person — this is the first people-leadership role. Learning to delegate, mentor, communicate expectations, and manage performance is a fundamentally different skill set than troubleshooting infrastructure. The transition is ongoing.

## Career Direction Uncertainty
Knows AI is the future and is building toward it, but the specific path isn't clear yet. "Get a job in AI" is the goal, but what kind of role? At what kind of company? The MSP world and the AI world don't obviously overlap in the job market.

## Feeling Behind
Started tech career late (~2017-2018 despite studying CS in 2013-2015). The feeling of being behind persists even though the actual output (ITAStack, homelab, Agent Zero) demonstrates advanced capability. This is more psychological than factual, but it drives both urgency and self-doubt.

## Balancing Building and Managing
The current role requires both technical delivery (projects, escalations, AI systems) and people management. These compete for time and mental energy. Building is the comfort zone; managing is the growth edge.

## Staying Ahead of AI
AI is moving fast. The tools, models, and capabilities change monthly. Staying current while also doing a full-time job requires aggressive prioritization and learning efficiency.
