# Personal Narratives

The stories James tells about himself and his journey.

## The Late Starter
Knew he wanted tech from a young age but didn't commit early. Started his tech career around 2017-2018 after studying CS at Mt. Hood Community College (2013-2015). Feels ~7-8 years in tech isn't enough. This narrative drives urgency but sometimes undervalues how far he's actually come.

**The tension**: James acknowledges he's likely getting ahead now, but the feeling of being behind is hard to shake. The output contradicts the narrative — in ~8 years he went from service desk to sysadmin to project engineer to operations manager and AI lead, built a 33-service integration platform, deployed multi-user AI agents, and runs a production K8s cluster. But feelings don't follow logic. This is an ongoing internal negotiation.

## The Technical Person Learning to Lead
Technical ability came naturally. People skills didn't. Moving into management and project leadership required learning a completely different skill set — communication, delegation, mentorship. Never Split the Difference was a turning point.

## The AI Pioneer at Work
Positioning himself as the person at his company (and in his career) who understands AI deeply enough to bridge it with business operations. Not just using AI tools — building AI systems that solve real problems.

## The Self-Hoster Turned Agent Operator
Testytech (homelab) isn't a hobby — it's a production lab and a statement of capability. Running 28+ services across Proxmox, K8s, and Docker demonstrates enterprise-level skills in a personal environment. But the narrative has evolved: James no longer manually manages infrastructure. He deployed Paperclip (11 autonomous AI agents handling SecOps, NetOps, StorageOps, MediaOps — 576+ tasks completed) and OpenClaw (8 scheduled cron jobs for zero-day monitoring, auto-remediation, and agent memory management). He now manages the agents that manage the infrastructure.
